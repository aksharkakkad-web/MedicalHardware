How this compares to a thermometer. A temporal-artery thermometer scans the forehead and keeps the peak reading, which is what this does. What it cannot copy is the resolution: one pixel covers about 3 cm at 1 m and 10 cm at 3 m, while a forehead is roughly 5 cm. Once a pixel is wider than a forehead, every reading blends skin with hair and background and reads low. A real thermometer also works at a fixed few centimetres with a calibrated skin-to-core offset; that offset changes with distance, airflow and ambient, so it is not applied here.

This is surface temperature, not body temperature. It reads whatever faces the sensor — clothing, hair, exposed skin — at whatever distance, with no emissivity calibration. Exposed skin normally sits around 88–95 °F against a 98.6 °F core. It cannot tell you whether someone has a fever or is healthy, and nothing here should be read that way.

CSI contributes movement, not body landmarks. Keypoint estimation from Wi-Fi needs several receive antennas to separate directions. This bench has one transmitter and one receiver, which is a single spatial path — enough to detect that the channel was disturbed, not enough to say where a limb is.
